All information contained herein is considered confidential and proprietary to ACME INC and to Avalon X.

This vulnerability scan report is designed to assist ACME INC in understanding their security posture, identifying potential security exposures, and prioritizing remediation efforts. The report includes actionable recommendations tailored to ACME INC’s specific environment, risk tolerance, and business requirements. The findings and recommendations are based on industry best practices, security frameworks, and external security assessment methodologies relevant to ACME INC’s infrastructure.

Section 01

Assessment Summary

1.1What This Scan Is (and Is Not)

What this scan is

  • A consent-based, external vulnerability scan
  • Focused on publicly exposed services and configurations
  • Intended to highlight potential areas for review
  • Suitable for regular monitoring and early risk awareness
  • Non-intrusive and designed to identify potential security hygiene issues

What this scan is not

  • Not a penetration test
  • Not an exploitative assessment
  • Not an internal network review
  • Not confirmation of compromise or breach
  • Not an assessment of application logic flaws
  • Not an authentication bypass test
1.2Overview

This report presents the results of a non-intrusive external vulnerability scan conducted against ACME INC. The purpose of this scan is to provide early visibility of potential external security exposures based on publicly accessible systems, configurations, and software versions. This assessment is designed to support informed security conversations and does not attempt to exploit vulnerabilities or disrupt live systems. It provides directional insight based on external observation only, not definitive risk ratings. The scan was conducted on 28 July 2026, and it highlights potential areas of interest for further review.

Section 02

Scope of Assessment

2.1Systems Tested

The assessment focused on the primary domain owned by ACME INC, encompassing publicly accessible web applications, email services, and DNS configurations. Key components included the CMS platform, SMTP services, and DNS records. The scope was limited to external-facing systems, excluding internal networks and applications. The production environment was assessed to ensure real-world applicability of findings.

2.2Testing Approach

This was a non-intrusive external vulnerability scan, not a penetration test. The approach included reconnaissance, service identification, version detection, and configuration review. Techniques such as external scanning, banner grabbing, and header analysis were employed. No exploitation was attempted, and findings are based on external observation rather than active testing.

2.3Standards and Frameworks

The assessment aligned with industry standards such as OWASP, NIST, and PTES. These frameworks guided the testing methodology, ensuring best practices were followed. The findings relate to common security recommendations, though compliance-specific assessments were not within the scope.

2.4Testing Timeline

The scan was conducted over a single day, 28 July 2026. Preparation and configuration took place in the morning, with active scanning occurring in the afternoon. Results were analysed and compiled into this report for delivery shortly thereafter.

2.5Scope Limitations

The assessment was limited to external systems, excluding internal applications and networks. Time constraints prevented exhaustive testing of all subdomains and services. Potential blind spots include areas outside the defined scope, such as internal configurations and non-publicly accessible systems.
